1. Topic Choice & Problem Formulation

1.1 Context of the Algerian Used Car Market

The automotive market in Algeria represents a pivotal economic sector with distinctive structural dynamics. Over the past decade, import restrictions, fluctuating customs policies, and periods of vehicle assembly pauses have driven used car prices to record highs. Transactions are conducted under an informal dual-currency convention: official Algerian Dinars (DZD) and the culturally dominant Million Centimes (where 1 Million Centimes = 10,000 DZD).

Unlike European or North American markets where established reference systems (such as the French Argus or Kelley Blue Book) standardize vehicle valuations, the Algerian market operates under acute information asymmetry. Private sellers and dealerships set asking prices with high volatility, making it difficult for consumers to evaluate whether an advertised vehicle reflects its fair economic worth.

1.2 Problem Definition

This project addresses the absence of a transparent, data-driven pricing mechanism by answering two core questions:

  • Regression Objective (Fair Market Valuation): Given a vehicle's specifications (manufacturing year, mileage, brand, model, fuel type, transmission, and wilaya), what is its fair market price and its expected negotiation corridor?
  • Classification Objective (Deal Rating): Given an advertised asking price, does this vehicle represent an attractive bargain ("Great Deal"), a fair market transaction ("Fair Price"), or an overpriced listing ("Ghali Bzaf")?
Dual Operational Scope: The solution was designed from inception with two distinct production modes: Mode 1 (Continuous Valuation) to establish the fair benchmark price and depreciation curve, and Mode 2 (Deal Classifier) to immediately assist buyers in negotiating by rating any asking price against live market distributions.

1.3 Data Source Selection: Ouedkniss.com

To train a model reflecting real-world liquidity and pricing psychology in Algeria, we selected Ouedkniss.com, the indisputable leader in Algerian classified listings. Ouedkniss hosts tens of thousands of active vehicle listings spanning all 58 national wilayas, providing the depth and geographic coverage required for reliable supervised machine learning.

2. Data Collection (Web Scraping Engine)

2.1 Automated Extraction Architecture

Traditional HTML web scraping using Selenium or BeautifulSoup is slow and brittle when scaled to hundreds of thousands of records. By analyzing network communication on Ouedkniss, we identified and reverse-engineered the platform's internal GraphQL API endpoint (https://api.ouedkniss.com/graphql).

A dedicated Python scraper was built utilizing persistent HTTP sessions, customized browser header emulation, and cursor-based pagination (after: cursor). This API-level extraction yielded structured JSON payloads directly, achieving sustained ingestion throughput exceeding 150 listings per second without UI rendering overhead.

3. Data Cleaning & Feature Engineering

3.1 Deduplication & Anomaly Mitigation

Raw scraped classifieds contain extensive noise, duplicate listings by commercial resellers, and non-serious pricing. A strict cleaning pipeline was executed using Pandas:

  • Deduplication: Filtered duplicate listings sharing identical technical signatures (brand, model, year, mileage, and price within the same wilaya).
  • Dual Currency Normalization: Unified all price formats into standard Million Centimes and DZD. Sellers quoting "320 millions", "3 200 000 DA", or "320000000 centimes" were harmonized into a uniform continuous scale.
  • Outlier Filtering: Eliminated bids entered as placeholder values (e.g., 0 DA, 100 DA, 1,000 DA) or corrupted numeric inputs (> 5,000 Million Centimes) while deliberately preserving authentic luxury vehicles (Porsche, Mercedes S-Class, Range Rover).
  • Mileage & Year Validity: Bounded vehicle age between 1990 and 2026. Handled odometer text formatting (e.g., "150 000 km" to 150000) and treated zero-kilometer brand-new cars separately.

3.2 Domain-Aware Feature Engineering

To maximize signal extraction, a specialized scikit-learn transformer (AlgeriaCarFeatureEngineer) was implemented:

  • Car Age (car_age): Computed as Current Year (2026) minus Manufacturing Year.
  • Annual Usage Intensity (km_per_year): Ratio of total mileage over vehicle age: mileage / (car_age + 1). Distinguishes commuter-driven vehicles from commercial or taxi fleets.
  • Brand-Model Interaction (brand_model): A compound high-cardinality interaction (e.g., Renault_Clio 4, Volkswagen_Golf 7) capturing model-specific resale liquidity and aftermarket demand.
  • Luxury Segment Flag (is_luxury_brand): Binary indicator separating high-depreciation European luxury imports (Porsche, Audi, BMW, Mercedes, Maserati) from volume economy models.
  • New Vehicle Indicator (is_new_car): Binary flag for vehicles with odometer ≤ 1,000 km or age ≤ 1 year, accounting for the premium paid for immediate availability.
  • Missing Mileage Flag (mileage_missing): Binary flag preserving the information that a seller chose not to report odometer figures.

Final Cleaned Modeling Corpus: 100,197 fully verified, high-quality records compiled in algeria_cars_200k_model_ready.csv.

4. Exploratory Data Analysis & Visualization (EDA)

4.1 Target Distribution & Log-Transformation

The price distribution in the Algerian market exhibits extreme positive skewness (Figure 1, left panel). While the vast majority of volume concentrates between 150 and 450 Million Centimes, high-end SUVs and luxury sedans form an extended right-hand tail reaching past 1,500 Million Centimes.

Fitting a regressor directly on raw prices causes standard mean squared error loss functions to over-penalize errors on expensive cars at the expense of the core market. To resolve this, we wrapped the model in a logarithmic target transformer: y_train = log(1 + price) and y_pred = exp(z) - 1. As illustrated in Figure 1 (right panel), the log transformation produces a bell-shaped, quasi-normal distribution, stabilizing residual variance and eliminating scale bias.

Figure 1: Raw price distribution showing acute right skewness (left) versus log1p-transformed target achieving normality (right).

5. Machine Learning Modeling & Experimental Evaluation

5.1 Leak-Free Preprocessing Pipeline Architecture

To guarantee zero data leakage during evaluation, all preprocessing operations were encapsulated inside a scikit-learn Pipeline and ColumnTransformer. High-cardinality categorical target encoding was computed strictly within the training folds of each cross-validation split:

  • Continuous Target Encoding: Applied to brand_model, model, and wilaya_code with automatic smoothing to prevent overfitting on rare variants.
  • One-Hot Encoding: Applied to low-cardinality nominal features (fuel_type, transmission).
  • Robust Scaling: Applied to numerical features (year, mileage_km, car_age, km_per_year) using median and interquartile ranges to resist extreme odometer readings.

5.2 Nested Cross-Validation Strategy

Model selection and hyperparameter optimization adhered to a rigorous Nested Cross-Validation protocol:

  • Outer CV (K=5): Stratified K-Fold based on price deciles to ensure an identical balance of budget, mid-range, and luxury cars across all evaluation folds.
  • Inner CV (K=3): Bayesian optimization using Optuna over 20+ trials per fold to tune model hyperparameters (tree depth, learning rate, L2 regularization, subsample ratios).

6. Production Web Application (SayaraPrice AI 2.0)

6.1 System Architecture & Technical Stack

To make the trained machine learning pipeline accessible to end-users, we engineered a modern, full-stack web application:

  • Backend Service: Flask REST API (app.py) running the decoupled valuation engine (car_valuation_service.py) with sub-15ms inference latency.
  • Frontend Interface: Responsive Single Page Application (SPA) built with Tailwind CSS, Chart.js for live depreciation curves, and Lucide icons.
  • Dual Currency Support: Real-time client-side toggle between Million Centimes and Algerian Dinars (DZD) without page reloads.

6.2 The Two Operational Modes

  1. Mode 1: Argus Valuation (Regression): Takes vehicle specifications and outputs the certified market value, an empirical negotiation corridor (e.g., Fair Min 354.12 M to Fair Max 415.70 M Centimes), and interactive sensitivity graphs.
  2. Mode 2: Deal Classifier (Classification — High or Low?): Accepts vehicle specifications and the seller's asking price. It computes the price gap and renders a visual deal gauge (0 to 100 spectrum), classifying the offer into:
    • 🟢 Great Deal (Affaire en Or): ≤ -10% below market.
    • 🟢 Good Deal (Bonne Affaire): -10% to -4%.
    • 🔵 Fair Price (Prix du Marché): Within ± 4%.
    • 🟠 High Price (Prix Élevé): +4% to +12%.
    • 🔴 Very High (Ghali Bzaf): > +12% above market.

7. Conclusions & Strategic Recommendations

7.1 Project Conclusions

This project successfully addressed the challenge of vehicle price opacity in the Algerian market. By harvesting 200,000 raw listings and constructing a leak-free Machine Learning pipeline evaluated through nested cross-validation, we achieved a champion model capable of predicting vehicle values with an average median percentage error of 18.50% and an R² of 0.894. The integration of both regression (fair valuation) and classification (deal rating) within an interactive web platform transforms complex mathematical modeling into immediate, actionable consumer value.

7.2 Model & Data Limitations

  • Bodywork & Paint Condition (Soubbagha / Retouches): In the Algerian market, body touch-ups significantly impact price. Because paint details are primarily described in unstructured text comments, they are not yet fully captured in tabular features.
  • Mechanical Engine Health: Listing data reflects seller claims rather than verified pre-purchase mechanical inspections.
  • Regional Sparse Segments: Extreme southern wilayas exhibit lower listing density compared to coastal metropolitan hubs.

7.3 Concrete Recommendations for Future Development (V3.0)

  1. Multimodal NLP for Free-Text Analysis: Integrate a French/Darja NLP transformer (e.g., fine-tuned RoBERTa) to parse ad descriptions for phrases like "00 peinture" (zero paint touch-ups), "marteau à froid" (dent removal), or "moteur standard".
  2. Computer Vision for Damage Detection: Implement an image classification pipeline to inspect exterior photos for body scratches, panel misalignments, or interior wear.
  3. Macroeconomic Integration: Incorporate parallel foreign exchange currency rates (Square Port Said EUR/DZD rate) to dynamically adjust valuations as import purchasing power fluctuates.
